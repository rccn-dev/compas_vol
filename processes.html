Processes

compas_vol.processes makes geometry by process: a rock is weathered, a coral is grown. The designer sets the mechanism and its rates; the form is what the mechanism leaves. Requires the processes extra (scikit-fmm).

from compas_vol.processes import Field, abrasion, pitting, strata

field = Field.from_object(massing, bounds=58.0, resolution=160)
field = strata(field, thickness=15.0, dip=(0.22, 0.08), depth=3.0)
field = pitting(field, depth=6.0, length=6.0)
rock = abrasion(field, depth=2.0).to_volume()       # a VolGrid, ready to model with

weathering

abrasion, differential, pitting, strata, runoff, jointing

growth

fingering, accretion, branching, diffusion_limited

generators

spinodal, reaction_diffusion, fbm noise

clean-up

opened (remove thin features), closed (fill narrow gaps)

One operator

Every process moves the surface along its normal at a speed that varies across it. On a signed distance field \(\phi\), negative inside:

\[\phi \leftarrow \phi + v \, |\nabla \phi|\]

the level-set equation of Osher and Sethian (1988). Positive \(v\) erodes, negative \(v\) grows. evolve() serves both; processes differ only in their speed terms, which compose:

from compas_vol.processes.terms import Convexity, Uniform

speed = Uniform(0.25) * (1.0 + 2.0 * Convexity(length=9.0))

Rates are millimetres per step; signals are dimensionless. A rate times signals is a rate.

Invariants

Every result is a distance field. A varying speed breaks \(|\nabla \phi| = 1\), and a 2 mm shell is only 2 mm thick if the field measures millimetres. Each step therefore ends in a sub-voxel redistance by fast marching (Sethian, 1996).

Every parameter is a length, including the length curvature is measured against. Measured against the voxel size, every curvature term vanishes and every process returns a blob.

The grid is the way out. to_volume() returns a VolGrid, which can be shelled, cut, filled with a lattice and exported like any other object.

Results are repeatable. Noise is a function of world coordinates, so a finer grid resolves the same noise. Every process takes a seed, and every result records its history:

>>> print(rock.provenance())
sample(source='VolEllipsoid(...)', bounds=(-58.0, ...), resolution=(160, 160, 160))
strata(thickness=15.0, dip=(0.22, 0.08), depth=3.0, ...)
pitting(depth=6.0, steps=12, length=6.0, ...)
abrasion(depth=2.0, steps=12, length=9.0, edge_bias=2.0)

Constraints

Some material must survive any process: a beam, a deck, a bearing face. constrain() states it once:

field = field.constrain(keep=beams, within=envelope, margin=150.0)

keep is solid that must remain; within is the envelope growth may not leave; margin fades a process out as it nears either. The constraint is applied at every redistance, so every process honours it without a parameter of its own, and violation() measures the result. Copies and crops keep it. examples/23_constraints.py shows a rib weathered with and without one.

Approximations

They model the shape a mechanism leaves:

  • There is no mass conservation, sediment transport or time in seconds. steps is a count; depths are millimetres of retreat.

  • Shelter is measured by blurring the solid; concavity stands in for moisture.

  • runoff() reads the model as a height field and ignores undersides.

  • spinodal() works on the voxel lattice; its feature size is converted from millimetres.

Accuracy and cost

Depth error is first order in the voxel size. A nominal 2 mm erosion of a sphere:

At 1603, a one-shot displacement such as strata() takes seconds, an iterated weathering 15-45 s, a hundred growth steps a few minutes. Features under about two voxels do not survive, so the process resolution sets the finest detail.

Unstable growth

Growth that runs faster where the surface bulges amplifies every wavelength, down to one voxel. fingering() controls it with curvature measured against a real length and a smoothed speed, whose smoothing length sets the fold width. When a speed exceeds half a voxel per step, evolve() scales the whole field down. Clipping each point would flatten the differences the instability depends on and turn folding into uniform growth.
