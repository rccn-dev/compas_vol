Introduction

A volumetric object is a function

\[f : \mathbb{R}^3 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}\]

returning the signed distance to its surface: negative inside, zero on the surface, positive outside. The surface is computed only when something has to be drawn or made.

Why signed distance functions

Operations on the object are operations on the function:

  • Booleans are min and max. They cannot fail on degenerate input or leave slivers.

  • Fillets and blends use a smooth minimum, with a chosen radius.

  • Offsets and shells are arithmetic on the distance.

  • Lattices and TPMS are periodic functions, evaluated only where needed.

  • Grading is a parameter that varies through space: wall thickness, cell size, even cell type.

The cost is meshing: a field is sampled on a grid and polygonised, which scales with the cube of the resolution. Modelling is exact; only the final surface is approximate.

How the package is organised

Every object subclasses compas_vol.base.VolObject and has two methods, tested to agree for every class:

get_distance(point)

The distance at one point.

get_distance_numpy(x, y, z)

The distance over a grid of np.ogrid arrays. Meshing and viewing use this one.
