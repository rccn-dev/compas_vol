segment

compas_vol.assembly.segment(obj, bounds, piece=(200.0, 200.0, 200.0), clearance=0.0, joint=None, resolution=None, cells=None)[source]

Cut a model into pieces small enough to make, and to compute.

Two problems, one cut. A pavilion does not fit on a print bed, a mill bed or a lorry; and it does not fit in memory either, which is the one that stops the work before it starts. A field has no size, so it costs nothing to describe a six-metre canopy – but sampling one at 1 mm voxels is 6000 cubed, 216 billion samples, 1.7 TB as float64. No machine does that, and no amount of patience helps.

Cut the same canopy into 400 mm pieces and each is 400 cubed at the same voxel size: 64 million samples, 512 MB, a couple of minutes. There are 750 of them, they are independent, and they can be built on different machines or on different days. The model is not approximated anywhere: each piece evaluates the same field, over a smaller box.

Parameters:

  • obj (compas_vol.base.VolObject) – The whole model.

  • bounds (compas.geometry.Box | float | sequence) – Its extent. See compas_vol.meshing.normalize_bounds().

  • piece (float | sequence[float]) – The largest piece allowed, per axis, in model units. Pieces come out no larger than this and are all equal along each axis, so a 1000 mm span at 400 mm gives three pieces of 333 mm, not two of 400 and one of 200.

  • clearance (float) – Gap left between neighbours, in model units, taken half from each side of every internal joint. This is the one number here a fabricator will ask for: zero means the pieces are modelled touching, and two parts modelled touching do not go together.

  • joint (compas_vol.assembly.Pins, optional) – Registration features across internal faces. Without one the pieces meet on flat cuts and have nothing locating them.

  • resolution (int, optional) – Only used when joint needs to test where material is. Defaults to a coarse probe, which is all a pin position needs.

  • cells (sequence, optional) – The pieces’ boxes, given directly instead of as a grid: each either bounds (xmin, ymin, zmin, xmax, ymax, zmax) or a dict with bounds and optionally name and role. This is how cuts follow the construction rather than a grid – the faces of beams, a panel size, a joint moved off an opening (place_cuts()). piece is ignored. Cells must not overlap; where two touch, that face gets the clearance and the joint, wherever it is and however much of it they share.

Returns:

Assembly

Notes

Pieces are cut on axis-aligned planes. That is the assumption worth knowing: a cut along a curved rib is not available. Cells that follow the structure along the axes are, and for a slab on a beam grid that is most of what a good cut is.

Examples

>>> from compas.geometry import Sphere
>>> from compas_vol.assembly import segment
>>> from compas_vol.primitives import VolSphere
>>> assembly = segment(VolSphere(Sphere(300.0)), bounds=320.0, piece=250.0)
>>> len(assembly)
27
>>> assembly.shape
(3, 3, 3)
>>> "{:.0f}".format(assembly.piece_size[0])
'213'
