Field.from_object

classmethod Field.from_object(obj, bounds, resolution=128, workers=None, redistanced=True, voxel=None)

Sample a volumetric object onto a grid.

Parameters:

  • obj (compas_vol.base.VolObject) – What to sample.

  • bounds (compas.geometry.Box | float | sequence) – The domain. See compas_vol.meshing.normalize_bounds(). Leave room around the model: a process that grows outward has nowhere to go otherwise, and anything touching the edge is cut there.

  • resolution (int | tuple[int, int, int]) – Samples along each axis. This is the resolution the process runs at, and it sets the finest feature any of them can produce: below about two voxels, nothing survives.

  • workers (int, optional) – Threads used for sampling. See compas_vol.meshing.sample().

  • redistanced (bool) – Rebuild the distance field after sampling. Most volumetric objects are not metric (a TPMS field, anything blended or multiplied), and processes need a true distance field to measure millimetres with.

  • voxel (float, optional) – Sample on a world lattice of this spacing instead of at resolution: the bounds grow outward to the nearest lattice planes, and every sample sits on a multiple of voxel. Two fields sampled this way over overlapping boxes share their samples exactly, which is what lets a model built piece by piece agree with itself where the pieces meet.

Returns:

Field

Examples

>>> from compas.geometry import Point, Sphere
>>> from compas_vol.primitives import VolSphere
>>> ball = VolSphere(Sphere(10, point=Point(0, 0, 0)))
>>> field = Field.from_object(ball, (-12.3, -12, -12, 12, 12, 12), voxel=2.0, redistanced=False)
>>> field.origin, field.spacing, field.shape
((-14.0, -12.0, -12.0), (2.0, 2.0, 2.0), (14, 13, 13))
