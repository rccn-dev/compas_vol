Assembly

A field costs nothing to describe at any size, but sampling it does: a 6 m canopy at 1 mm voxels is 216 billion samples. compas_vol.assembly cuts a model into pieces that each fit a machine and fit in memory.

Cutting

from compas_vol.assembly import Pins, segment

assembly = segment(model, bounds, piece=400.0, clearance=0.4,
                   joint=Pins(diameter=8.0, depth=5.0, fit=0.15))

Each Piece is the model intersected with its box. clearance comes off every shared face; Pins adds a pin and a socket across each, only where both sides have material.

piece gives a grid. cells gives any non-overlapping boxes instead, so cuts can follow the construction: beam faces, panel sizes. Faces two cells share, including T-junctions, get the clearance and the joints. place_cuts() moves a cut, within a tolerance, to where section_cost() says it meets the most material.

A piece cut from a lattice is often several parts: struts held by material outside the box come loose. check() finds them; keep_largest() drops them.

Building piece by piece

A process reads the surface around each point, so pieces processed separately must still agree where they meet:

from compas_vol.assembly import Seams

seams = Seams(assembly, step=2.0)
for piece, finished, processed in assembly.build(weather, voxel=2.0, halo=16.0, seams=seams):
    ...                                    # one piece in memory at a time
print(seams.summary())

  • Halo. Each piece is processed over its box grown by halo, so the process sees the neighbour’s material at a cut, then cut back. Make the halo longer than any length the process measures with.

  • Lattice. Pieces are sampled on a world lattice of spacing voxel, so overlapping halos share their samples exactly.

  • Coherent noise. Noise is a function of world coordinates, so every piece sees the same noise. A per-piece simulation such as spinodal() grows a different result in each box.

Seams compares neighbours on each shared face: the gap between their surfaces, and the share of the face that one side calls solid and the other void. cost() quotes the whole model against the largest piece at a given voxel and halo.

examples/26_piece_by_piece.py is a small case. grid-roof builds 53 pieces whose seams meet within about 2 mm at 40 mm voxels.
